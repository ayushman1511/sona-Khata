```
2.2 Gold given to karigars for job-work is tracked on trust, and disputes follow 
AGENTIC AI 
The problem. Jewellers issue gold to artisan goldsmiths by weight and receive finished pieces back with agreed 
wastage. Tracking issue, return, wastage, deadlines and payments across many karigars and custom orders is 
manual, which causes losses, disputes, missed delivery dates and late payment to artisans. 
Who lives with this. These are karigars working from small workshops or home for several jewellers at once, and 
the jewellers juggling custom orders across many of them. 
How it gets handled today. Issues are noted in a notebook or on a slip, wastage is agreed verbally, deadlines are 
chased by phone, and accounts are squared up at month end. 
What it costs. It costs unexplained gold losses, disputes that sour long relationships, missed wedding dates and 
artisans waiting weeks to be paid. 
Who else is in the picture. 
1. Jewellers and their counter staff 
2. Karigars and their helpers 
3. Customers with fixed occasion dates 
4. Refiners who recover gold from workshop scrap 
What makes it hard. Accuracy has to hold at the level of weight, the audit trail has to be one both parties accept, 
and fairness matters most to the artisan, who has less power. 
Boundaries. Settlements go to both parties for approval, and each karigar has full access to his own account. 
Questions to take into the field. 
1. How is gold issued and recorded today, and who holds the record? 
2. How is wastage agreed, and how often is it disputed? 
3. What happens when a piece is late for a wedding? 
4. How long does a karigar wait to be paid after delivery? 
5. What does a karigar do when the jeweller's number differs from his own? 
6. How many karigars and open orders does a typical shop handle at once? 
Following the money. A karigar with a clean record of work delivered looks very different to anyone deciding 
whether to trust him.
```

## General expectations that apply to every statement (page 3, verbatim)

```
What we expect you to build 
Most statements are marked Agentic AI. These call for a system that pursues a goal over many steps on 
behalf of its user: it plans, acts in external systems, keeps memory of the case, recovers when something fails 
and seeks human approval before any action involving money, legal commitment or personal data. 
Sandboxed or simulated integrations are allowed as long as they are disclosed, and the bar is a system that 
acts for its user, well beyond answering questions. 
Statements marked Applied AI Systems put the depth of the model, data or optimisation at the core, whether 
that is vision on commodity hardware, speech in Indian languages, forecasting under uncertainty, risk 
modelling, optimisation or market design. These are judged on measured performance. 
Conditions every solution works within 
The people in these statements speak their own languages and often mix them, many have limited literacy, 
most use entry-level Android phones, and connectivity comes and goes. Solutions are expected to be voice-
first and vernacular, to hold up in those conditions, and to offer an assisted path through a JanAI Café 
wherever documents, authentication or physical presence are needed. Consent is explicit, data collection 
stays limited to what the task needs, and mock integrations are clearly labelled wherever public or banking 
systems lack open interfaces. 
Evaluation 
Submissions are judged on two dimensions. The technical dimension covers architecture, quality of AI, a 
working prototype and innovation, while the entrepreneurial dimension covers problem relevance, market, 
business model, scalability and job creation. Final criteria and weightages are published with the registration 
details.
```
